# 00 — Overview, Scope & Output Contract

## Goal
Ranked **Top-5 tour lists** for the **50 most-visited countries** (by international tourist arrivals, latest UN Tourism data), organised **by theme-within-region**. The unit of work is a **theme**, not a country.

## What a "theme" is
A focused regional experience that can be done as a single trip (kept **< 21 days**), built around **one coherent subject** (history, archaeology, art, design, food, religion, wildlife/nature, etc.). A theme may span multiple **eras** (Sicily's layered Greek→Roman→Arab-Norman→Baroque civilisations) or multiple **regions** (Etruscan Italy, Magna Graecia) — that is still one subject. But it must NOT bundle multiple **lenses** (history vs food vs coast) to look comprehensive: that is breadth-over-depth and it dilutes the expert. Different lenses split into different themes (Sicily layered civilisations and Etna food & wine are two themes, not one). Examples:
- India → South Indian temple trail (Tamil Nadu); North Indian Mughal history (Delhi–Agra–Rajasthan); Kerala food & backwaters; Ladakh Buddhist culture; Varanasi–Ganges spiritual.
- Italy → Rome & classical antiquity; Florence/Tuscany Renaissance art; Sicily layered civilisations; Naples–Pompeii; Venetian art & lagoon; Emilia-Romagna food.
- Kenya → regional safari circuits (Masai Mara vs Amboseli/Tsavo), led by top naturalist guides — wildlife is the standout lens, not archaeology.

Big, diverse countries get many themes; small/single-note destinations get one or two. Match each theme to what the region is **genuinely best known for**.

## Two consumption modes (why the theme is single-lens)
The output serves two different needs — keep them separate:
1. **Group-tour ranking (this method's core deliverable).** The **theme is always single-lens** because depth + a genuinely expert guide only hold when the trip commits to one subject. A multi-lens group tour is breadth-over-depth and the expert degrades to a generalist coach. So the Top-5 ranking unit is single-lens, full stop.
2. **Trip composition (the traveller's actual journey).** A tourist legitimately wants multi-lens — e.g. Sicily = layered-civilisations days + an Etna-wine day + coast. That is built by **combining several ranked themes**, not by widening one. Whole-trip multi-lens forfeits a single expert; depth is recovered only via **per-segment specialists** (take each theme's ranked expert, a generalist glues the gaps) or a **bespoke designer (channel F)** who sequences multiple theme-experts into one private itinerary (multi-lens *with* expertise, at a price for the orchestration).

This method produces the single-lens **building blocks**; the multi-lens itinerary is a composition layer on top (see `11-trip-composition.md`). The method never bundles lenses to fake comprehensiveness.

## Theme design rules
- **Non-overlapping by tour product.** A region with two genuine lenses (Tuscany = Renaissance art AND wine/food) gets two themes — different tours, no double-count.
- **Cover every region.** A region with zero themes must be explicitly tested-and-justified, not assumed empty.
- **First-trip-representative.** Iconic and deep, not hyper-niche.

## Ranking criteria (priority order)
1. **Expert guide fit** — a genuinely expert, highly regarded leader for the theme (real historian, Egyptologist, naturalist/tracker, food or religion specialist) — not a figurehead or generic coach guide.
2. **Depth within the theme** — goes beneath the surface; not a checklist.
3. **Small groups, authentic, locally connected** — private-departure option a plus.
4. **Value for money** — price is not a barrier; the best option wins even if pricier. But do NOT reward price/luxury for its own sake: cost must be justified by quality/expertise/depth. When two tours are comparably excellent, the better value ranks higher. Explicitly flag any tour charging a large premium for thin substance.

Constant test: *"best for a first-time visitor to this theme/region — deep, expert-led, authentic, with cost justified by what it delivers."*

## Output contract — per country → per theme
- Theme ID (per the `06` convention, e.g. `IT-01`) + country (with arrivals rank) + theme/region, with a one-line statement of what the theme captures.
- A ranked **Top 5 tours** (5 is a **ceiling, not a quota** — list 3 if only 3 clear the bar, and say why; never pad), each with: operator · tour name · the guide/expertise leading it · group size · duration · approximate price · a brief value-for-money note · the specific depth/access feature earning its rank · source link.
- **Price convention:** quote in the operator's **listed currency** (state which) plus a rough **USD-equivalent** so the value comparison across the Top-5 is like-for-like.
- **Format-class flag:** if the Top-5 mixes fixed-departure group / private-bespoke / day-format products, flag it so the reader compares like with unlike knowingly (see `07`).
- One line on **why #1 wins** for that theme.
- **Flag** any tour where the leader or a current 2026–2027 departure couldn't be verified — do not guess.

## Hard rules
- Do not invent guides, dates, prices, or claims. Unverified → say so.
- Prioritise tours operating in the current/next season (2026–2027). The season **rolls** — re-baseline it on the freshness cadence (`08`); don't let "2026–2027" silently go stale.
- If a theme has weak organised options, **say so** and give the closest strong fits rather than padding.
- Cite sources; every factual claim traceable to a live URL.
